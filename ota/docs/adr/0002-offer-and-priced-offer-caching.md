# OADR-0002: Offer and priced-offer caching and expiry

**Status:** Accepted
**Date:** 2026-10-12
**Deciders:** OTA backend tech lead. Decided during planning; revisit when search volume makes PostgreSQL inserts of offers a bottleneck (more than about 50 searches per second), or when an asynchronous search mode is needed.
**Revisions:** 2026-11-19 (lead decision (30d) of 2026-11-19, approved by the owner; written by FIX-W61-PLAN, applied by OBE-105 after the launch): point 6's purge also trims an expired search that a checkout or a booking holds, once every checkout that holds it is final: it deletes the search's offers, upsells and priced offers that no checkout or booking needs, directly or through the rows they were priced from, and keeps the search row and every row that a reader of a checkout or a booking needs; the `RESTRICT` foreign keys stay the safety net, and a marker on the search records the trim (dated note under point 6; Follow-ups). Until OBE-105 is merged, point 6 applies as first written. 2026-11-19 (lead decisions (31c), (31d) and (31e) of 2026-11-19, approved by the owner; FIX-W62-DOCS): the lead accepted three points of that revision as written: the trim's marker is a schema delta over the frozen reference SQL, which data model §3 documents when OBE-105 adds it; the deleted rows of a trimmed search answer `404` and OBE-105 does not keep the offers its highlights name; and no retention period is set now for checkouts that never became bookings (dated line under point 6's revision; Follow-ups).

## Context

The contract stores every search and offer (`flight_searches`, `flight_offers`, `offer_upsells`, `priced_offers`) with an `expires_at`, hands out capability ids (`srch_`, `off_`, `prc_`) and filters results in SQL on summary columns ([db README §2.1, §6, §10](../../../docs/velexis-sdk/db/README.md)). It offers a synchronous and an asynchronous search mode, and re-prices at completion when a priced offer is older than `repriceAfterMinutes` (default 10). Amadeus offers are only valid for a short time and fares change. On one VM with one Redis and `noeviction`, large result sets in Redis would compete with queues. We need to fix where offers live, how long each object is valid, when we re-price, and what the purge job does.

## Decision

1. **PostgreSQL is the offer store.** Searches, offers, upsells and priced offers are rows in the tenant schema, inserted in one transaction per search (multi-row insert, at most 250 offers). Redis holds no offer data.
2. **Redis only for single flight.** Identical concurrent searches of one tenant and environment (same normalised criteria hash) share one Amadeus call through `t:<tenantId>:<env>:search:<sha256>` (30 s): the second request waits up to 30 s for the first search id and returns its results. No result cache across visitors beyond that.
3. **Lifetimes.**

   | Object | `expires_at` |
   |---|---|
   | Search and its offers | `now + storefront_settings.offer_ttl_minutes` (default 30) |
   | Upsell offers | Same as the parent search |
   | Priced offer | `min(now + offer_ttl_minutes, end of lastTicketingDate)` |
   | Checkout | `now + checkout_ttl_minutes` (default 30), +15 min when a payment is created |

   Reads after expiry answer `410 SEARCH_EXPIRED` / `OFFER_EXPIRED`, as in the contract.
4. **Re-price rules.** `checkout.complete` (and the airline-card completion) re-prices with Flight Offers Price when the current priced offer is expired, older than 10 minutes, or travellers carry discount or loyalty data. A higher total stops the booking with `409 PRICE_CHANGED`; a lower one is accepted with a warning. `checkout.create` itself does not re-price.
5. **Selling prices are fixed at insert time.** Markups and fees are computed when the offer or priced offer row is written ([OADR-0005](0005-markup-and-fee-engine.md)); a rule change applies to the next search, never to stored offers.
6. **Purge.** `offers.purge` every 15 minutes per tenant deletes expired searches (cascading to offers, upsells and priced offers) **unless** a checkout references one of their priced offers (`priced_offer_id` or `base_priced_offer_id`; the `RESTRICT` foreign keys are the safety net). Deletes run in batches of 500 searches to keep transactions short.

   **2026-11-19 (lead decision (30d) of 2026-11-19, approved by the owner; written by FIX-W61-PLAN, applied by OBE-105 after the launch): the purge trims the searches that checkouts and bookings hold.** OBE-104's samplers found that this point keeps the whole answer behind every checkout for good. A search stays while a checkout references one of its priced offers, and no job deletes checkouts (they stay with the bookings as records, [data model §6](../data-model.md#6-retention)). So every checkout, paid or abandoned, keeps all 150 offers of its search (up to 250), though it bought one: 0.34 to 0.48 MiB of `flight_offers` per checkout, about 12 to 17 GiB a year at 100 checkouts a day. Of the lines the offers and the supplier log add to the database, it is the only one without a bound ([`performance.md`](../performance.md) §11, "A finding for the lead"). Until OBE-105 is merged the point applies as first written, and the capacity watch of [deployment §14](../../../docs/mvp/deployment-single-vm.md#14-capacity-limits-of-one-vm) includes the size of `flight_offers`.
   - **Held searches.** A search is *held* while a checkout or a booking references one of its priced offers (`checkouts.priced_offer_id`, `checkouts.base_priced_offer_id`, `bookings.priced_offer_id`), or while one of its priced offers has not expired (a priced offer may outlive its search by up to the offer lifetime, point 3). An expired search that is not held is deleted as before, cascading to its offers, upsells and priced offers. The bookings' column joins the condition because the purge now deletes priced offers one by one: a booking's priced offer can differ from its checkout's for a while (after an accepted re-price, checkout.complete records the new priced offer on the booking before Flight Create Orders is sent and moves the checkout only once the order may exist: `recordSending` and `moveCheckout` in `booking-orchestrator.service.ts`), and `bookings.priced_offer_id` is `ON DELETE SET NULL`, not `RESTRICT`.
   - **When a held search is trimmed.** Once: when it has expired, none of its priced offers is unexpired, and every checkout that references one of them is final (`completed`, `expired` or `abandoned`). From then on no step adds a priced offer to it: flights.price and flights.upsell refuse its expired offers, and only an open checkout re-prices (checkout.setAncillaries, the re-price of point 4). A checkout still open keeps the whole search until then, as today. The trim is recorded on the search row, in a nullable timestamp that a tenant migration of OBE-105 adds to `flight_searches` (no backfill, [conventions §9](../../../docs/mvp/conventions.md#9-database-migrations): the searches held before it are trimmed by the purge's first runs, oldest first). A trimmed search stays held for good, because no job deletes the checkouts and bookings that hold it, so neither part of a later run reads it again: the delete of unheld searches skips it by its marker without testing its hold, as the trim does (until a later change ends the hold, Follow-ups).
   - **What a trim keeps** (the *kept rows*), decided for each search when it is trimmed:
     1. every priced offer that a checkout or a booking references (the three columns above), and every priced offer that one was priced from, through `parent_priced_offer_id`, as far as the chain goes (the base of a checkout.setAncillaries child, the priced offer that a checkout.complete re-price started from);
     2. every offer named in a kept priced offer's `offer_ids`. `offer_id` is the first of them: `PricedOffersRepository.byId` reaches the search through it with an inner join, and `PricedOffersService.pricedParts` reads all of them and fails for a missing one. `offer_ids` has no foreign key, so the `RESTRICT` keys do not protect these offers;
     3. for each kept offer that an upsell returned (`origin = 'upsell'`), its `offer_upsells` row (the answer's dictionaries, `baseOfferId` and `upsellId`) and that upsell's base offer (`offer_upsells.base_offer_id`, `ON DELETE CASCADE`: deleting it would delete the upsell and the kept offer with it), and so on while the base offer is itself an upsold offer;
     4. the offers of the same search whose Amadeus ids a kept offer names in `upsellFlightOfferIds` (in its stored payload or in the confirmed offers of its priced offer's stored answer): the PricedOffer that checkout.get answers translates them into `upsellOfferIds` through `OffersRepository.searchOfferIds`, so they must not disappear from it. A handful at most;
     5. the search row itself, unchanged: `byId`'s inner join, the dictionaries and highlights in the answers of its kept offers, flights.getSearch (which still answers an expired search, with `status: "expired"` and the row's facets, highlights, dictionaries and one-way combinations; console scope adds the diagnostics of its latest supplier call), and `supplier_requests.search_id` (`ON DELETE SET NULL`), which the console's supplier log shows and filters by.

     Everything else of a trimmed search is deleted: its other offers, search results and upsold ones; their upsells; and every priced offer that is not kept (other pricings of the same offers, a re-price that a higher total refused, the ancillary children that a later checkout.setAncillaries replaced).
   - **The readers the kept rows serve.** checkout.get and every checkout step that answers the checkout (`PricedOffersService.forCheckout`: the priced offer, its offers with their search and upsell); checkout.setAncillaries and its catalogue (the base priced offer); checkout.createPayment and checkout.updatePayment (`pricedOfferOf` in `payments.service.ts`); checkout.complete and the airline-card completion (the priced offer, the base, and the re-price of point 4, which sends the kept offers' verbatim payloads to Flight Offers Price); the reconciliation of a lost Create Orders answer (the stored answer of the booking's priced offer, for the flights of the e-mail it sends); bookings.cancel and console.bookings.cancel (the non-refundable fees of the booking's priced offer); bookings.update (the contact requirements of the booking's priced offer); the console's booking view (`pricedOfferId`, which a deleted priced offer would set to null); flights.getSearch (item 5); and the console's supplier log (`searchId`). The checkout steps that change a checkout run only while it is open, before any trim; checkout.get, flights.getSearch, the booking readers and the console read after it. A deleted row is answered as the rows of a search the purge deletes whole are today: flights.getOffer, flights.price, flights.upsell, flights.getPricedOffer (with its ancillaries and fare rules) and checkout.create answer `404` where the expired row answered `410 OFFER_EXPIRED`, and the offer ids in the search's highlights and one-way combinations may name deleted offers. The booking pages (`bookings.get`, and `bookings.getFareRules` from the order), the booking documents, the other booking e-mails, the erasure job and the calls of `booking-load.mjs` (made before their search expires) read none of these tables.
   - **Batching.** One job first deletes a batch of expired searches that are neither held nor trimmed (500, as before), then trims a batch of held searches (at most 100 a statement, 15 000 to 25 000 offers at 150 to 250 a search, fewer rows than one batch of deletes), each batch in its own short transaction, oldest expiry first, the searches locked `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`. A full batch of either asks for a continuation as before (`MAX_CONTINUATIONS`). The job's counts and its `offers.purged` line add the searches trimmed and the offers, upsells and priced offers they lost: counts only, never an id.
   - **The safety net.** The `RESTRICT` foreign keys of `checkouts.priced_offer_id` and `checkouts.base_priced_offer_id` stay. A trim that would delete a priced offer a checkout references fails the statement: the job fails, BullMQ tries it again, and the next attempt decides the kept rows again, as a checkout created between the choice and the delete does today. The keys do not cover `offer_ids`, the offers of item 4 or `bookings.priced_offer_id` (`SET NULL`), so OBE-105's integration tests read each of the readers above after a purge.
   - **Measured** by OBE-105 with `booking-load.mjs`, before and after, as OBE-101 measured its bound: the rows and MiB of `flight_offers` (and of `flight_searches`) kept per checkout, against §11's 150 rows and 0.34 to 0.48 MiB.
   - *Note (2026-11-19, lead decisions (31c), (31d) and (31e), approved by the owner, FIX-W62-DOCS):* three points of this revision are accepted as written. (31c) OBE-105's tenant migration `0006`, which adds the nullable marker `flight_searches.offers_trimmed_at` with no backfill, is a schema delta over the frozen reference SQL ([`tenant.sql`](../../../docs/velexis-sdk/db/tenant.sql)); OBE-105 documents it in [data model](../data-model.md) §3 when it adds it, as the deltas of `0002` to `0005` are. (31d) After a trim, the deleted offers and priced offers of a held search answer `404` instead of `410 OFFER_EXPIRED` on flights.getOffer, flights.price, flights.upsell, flights.getPricedOffer and checkout.create, exactly as for the searches the purge deletes whole, and a trimmed search's highlights and one-way combinations may name deleted offers: accepted as is, so OBE-105 does not keep the offers the highlights name. (31e) No retention period is set now for checkouts that never became bookings: OBE-105's trim bounds the growth, and ending their hold stays an option in the Follow-ups, to revisit after the launch with real volumes.
7. **Synchronous search only in the MVP.** `resultOptions.async: true` answers `403 FEATURE_DISABLED` (`details.feature = "asyncSearch"`). The hosted storefront waits for the result with a progress state; the 30 s Amadeus timeout and the SDK's 60 s timeout fit.
8. **Pagination stays in SQL** within one search (`flights.listResults`), sorted by the indexed summary columns; facets and highlights are computed once at insert and stored on the search row.

## Consequences

### Positive

- One store, transactional with the selling-price computation; capability ids survive API restarts and work across processes.
- Redis memory stays small and predictable, which `noeviction` requires.
- The purge is a plain indexed delete; checkouts in progress are never broken by it.

### Negative

- Each search writes up to 250 rows with large jsonb payloads; WAL and table bloat grow with traffic. Autovacuum and the 15-minute purge keep it bounded at MVP volumes. *2026-11-19:* not the searches that checkouts hold, which point 6 as first written keeps whole for good (0.34 to 0.48 MiB of offers per checkout, [`performance.md`](../performance.md) §11); its revision of 2026-11-19 bounds them (OBE-105, after the launch).
- No cross-visitor result cache: two visitors searching the same route a minute apart cost two Amadeus calls. Shared-quota buckets protect Amadeus.
- No asynchronous mode for third-party serverless frontends in the MVP.

### Follow-ups

- Measure search insert time and table sizes in the M2 load test; consider partitioning `flight_offers` by day (db README §9) only if needed.
- Asynchronous search (queue `ota-searches`) when a third-party frontend needs it.
- *2026-11-19 (lead decision (30d)).* OBE-105 applies point 6's revision after the launch, a named launch exception ([ADR-0025](../../../docs/mvp/adr/0025-roadmap-scheduling-and-tracking.md) item 4): the purge job, its unit and integration tests, the tenant migration of the trim's marker, [data model](../data-model.md) §3 and §6, and an errata candidate for the frozen [db README](../../../docs/velexis-sdk/db/README.md) §6 row on the offer cache purge (in [`ota/backend/openapi/README.md`](../../backend/openapi/README.md) §7). Until then [deployment §14](../../../docs/mvp/deployment-single-vm.md#14-capacity-limits-of-one-vm) watches the size of `flight_offers`.
- *2026-11-19, an option that is not decided:* ending the hold of checkouts that never became bookings (`expired` or `abandoned`) after a retention period, so that their searches, the search row and the kept rows included, are deleted as well. It needs a retention period for those checkouts, which no document sets: [data model §6](../data-model.md#6-retention) lists no purge of checkouts, and the erasure job keeps a deleted customer's checkouts, detached, with their amounts. It would also mean deleting those checkout rows (with their travellers, contacts, ancillaries and remarks) or detaching their priced offers, whose foreign keys are `NOT NULL` in the reference schema. A question for the lead and the owner; after OBE-105 such a checkout keeps a few rows, not its search's whole answer. *Decided for now (2026-11-19, lead decision (31e), approved by the owner, FIX-W62-DOCS):* no retention period is set; OBE-105's trim bounds the growth, and this option stays here, to revisit after the launch with real volumes.
- *2026-11-19, an option for OBE-105's measurement:* a trimmed search's own row keeps its `facets`, `highlights`, `dictionaries` and `one_way_combinations`. After the search expires, flights.listResults answers `410 SEARCH_EXPIRED`, but flights.getSearch still answers the search (`status: "expired"`) with all four, and the answers of its kept offers read the dictionaries and highlights. Clearing any of them would change those answers, so it is a later change of this point and of flights.getSearch's answer, only if OBE-105 measures that the kept search rows matter.

## Alternatives considered

- **Offers in Redis with a TTL, only priced offers in PostgreSQL.** Faster writes, but SQL filtering, capability ids across restarts and the contract's `x-tenant-tables` all assume rows; Redis memory would grow with traffic.
- **A cross-visitor search cache (e.g. 5 minutes per route and date).** Saves quota but serves stale availability and complicates per-visitor currency and markups.
- **Re-price on every checkout step.** More Amadeus calls for little gain; the completion re-price catches changes where they matter.
