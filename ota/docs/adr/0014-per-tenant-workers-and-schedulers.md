# OADR-0014: Per-tenant workers and schedulers

**Status:** Accepted
**Date:** 2026-10-12
**Deciders:** OTA backend tech lead. Decided during planning; revisit when more than about 300 active tenants make the per-tenant fan-out slow, or when per-group fairness needs BullMQ Pro.
**Revisions:** 2026-10-22 (lead decision (1), recording FIX-W26-BOOKINGS): point 6's claims lock the booking with `FOR NO KEY UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`, never `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`, and the booking documents serialise their own writes without a row lock on the booking (dated note under point 6). 2026-10-23 (lead decision (3), recording FIX-W27-SYNC): the in-progress marker of the booking sync's claim, which the refreshes share, is a column of the booking, not a booking event (dated note under point 6). 2026-10-23 (lead decision (8), second batch, recording FIX-W28-FOLLOWUPS): the sync's and the refreshes' claims are tried again after 30 minutes, while the other claims of point 6 count as abandoned after 5 (dated note under point 6). 2026-10-26 (lead decision (1), recording FIX-W30-OTA): bookings.cancel refuses a booking with a fresh ticketing claim, as console.bookings.cancel does (dated note under point 6). 2026-10-27 (lead decision (1), recording FIX-W31-OTA): the other way round, the ticketing claims (the ticketing job's and console.bookings.issue's) leave a booking alone from the commit of a cancellation's claim until its outcome is recorded, and while a DELETE without an answer is undecided (dated note under point 6); a Consequence follows. 2026-10-28 (lead decision (1), recording FIX-W32-OTA): checkout.complete's own issuance is the third ticketing claim that respects a cancellation's hold; bookings.cancel and console.bookings.cancel refuse while another cancellation's claim is fresh, and every outcome step clears only its own claim's marker; a cancellation the worker hands to the staff's review keeps ticketing held until the staff settle it, confirmed as built (dated note under point 6); the Consequences follow.

## Context

The OTA worker runs the jobs of [db README §6](../../../docs/velexis-sdk/db/README.md): checkout expiry, offer purge, booking sync, ticketing retries, notification outbox, queue polling, retention purges, plus the MVP's reconciliation and Stripe event processing. [ADR-0009](../../../docs/mvp/adr/0009-background-jobs-bullmq.md) fixes BullMQ on the single Redis, job schedulers declared at start-up, small per-tenant jobs for fairness, bounded concurrency, ids-only payloads and idempotent handlers. Suspended tenants keep obligations to paid travellers ([PADR-0014](../../../platform/docs/adr/0014-tenant-suspension-and-resume.md)). The worker has a 512 MB memory budget ([architecture §3](../../../docs/mvp/architecture.md#3-hosts-ports-and-the-vm)).

## Decision

1. **Schedulers fan out, jobs do the work.** Each scheduler (`upsertJobScheduler`, declared in `jobs/schedulers.ts`) runs one small job that lists target tenants from the cached platform data (`status IN ('active','suspended')`), filters by what applies, and enqueues one job per tenant (or per booking) with a deterministic job id (`<task>:<tenantId>:<minute bucket>`), so overlapping runs do not duplicate work.
2. **Work per job is bounded**: at most 20 bookings for sync, 50 checkouts for expiry, 500 searches for purge, 100 outbox rows for notifications. A job that finds more re-enqueues itself.
3. **Queues and concurrency** are those of [`../architecture.md`](../architecture.md) §6 (2 to 4 per queue). Total concurrent jobs stay below the worker's database pool (10 connections) and memory budget.
4. **Suspended tenants**: checkout expiry, ticketing, sync, reconciliation, refunds, Stripe events and notifications for existing bookings continue; the queue poller and anything that starts new supplier activity skip them. `provisioning`, `failed` tenants are skipped entirely.
5. **Every processor** opens `tenantTx` itself (the job's tenant id is resolved to the schema through the same cache as the API), re-checks the current state of its rows, and exits early when nothing is due. Handlers never trust the payload beyond ids.
6. **Claims instead of long locks.** Work on one booking (ticketing, sync, reconcile, cancel money step) first claims it in a short transaction with `SELECT … FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`, by writing an in-progress marker (for example `ticketing_status = 'in_progress'` and `ticketing_last_attempt_at = now()`, or `reconcile_attempts + 1`), and commits before any external call. Other workers and API requests (such as `console.bookings.issue`) skip or refuse a booking with a fresh claim; a claim older than 5 minutes counts as abandoned. The booking's `version` detects concurrent changes when the result is written.

   **2026-10-22 (lead decision (1), FIX-W26-BOOKINGS): the claim's lock mode.** Every claim of a booking (the ticketing job's and the reconciliation's inline issuance, the reconciliation's claim and the scan's adoption of stale claims, the booking sync of OBE-045, and any later `SKIP LOCKED` on `bookings`) locks it with `SELECT … FOR NO KEY UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`, never `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`. The insert of a row that references a booking (a booking event, an outbox row, a booking document, a ticket, a guest token, a refund) takes the booking's `FOR KEY SHARE` through its foreign key; `FOR UPDATE` conflicts with it, so a claim made in that moment skipped the booking and left it to the next scan (the ticketing scan runs every 5 minutes: a booking whose itinerary was being written stayed `confirmed` that long, commit 16c3ea9). `FOR NO KEY UPDATE` does not conflict with `FOR KEY SHARE`, and still conflicts with itself, `FOR SHARE` and `FOR UPDATE`, so a claim still skips a booking another claim or a writer that locked the booking holds. No claim's correctness relies on excluding key-share holders: a key-share holder can only add rows that reference the booking (or point a row at it), never change the booking row a claim reads and writes (it would need `FOR NO KEY UPDATE` or more, which waits for the claim); the ticketing and reconciliation results are written later in a transaction that locks the booking `FOR UPDATE` again, waits for such inserts and checks the claim's fencing token, and the adoption writes only the booking row and its own event. The claim's own update must leave the booking's key columns (`id`, `reference`, `checkout_id`) alone, or it would take `FOR UPDATE` itself. The booking documents never lock a booking `FOR UPDATE` (nor `FOR NO KEY UPDATE` or `FOR SHARE`): their writes of one booking are serialised by a transaction-level advisory lock keyed on the booking id (`pg_advisory_xact_lock`), and they read the booking's `version` `FOR KEY SHARE` and compare it with the version they rendered ([`../architecture-booking.md`](../architecture-booking.md) §1.1).

   **2026-10-23 (lead decision (3), FIX-W27-SYNC): the sync's in-progress marker.** The booking sync's claim, and the refreshes' (bookings.get with a refresh, console.bookings.get with `refresh=true`, console.bookings.resync), write their in-progress marker in a column, `bookings.sync_claimed_at`, as the ticketing job writes `ticketing_last_attempt_at`: never a booking event (until then a staff-only `booking.sync_requested`, about four a day per upcoming booking and one an hour near the ticketing deadline, which filled the console's timeline). The sync skips a booking claimed in the last 30 minutes (a failed read is tried again then), a refresh is throttled by the same column; the claim's update leaves `version`, `updated_at` and the key columns alone.

   **2026-10-23 (lead decision (8), second batch, FIX-W28-FOLLOWUPS): how long a claim holds.** The claims of a synchronisation, the booking sync's and the refreshes' (`bookings.sync_claimed_at`), are tried again after 30 minutes (`BOOKING_SYNC_RETRY_MS`, `bookings/sync-schedule.ts`), not after this point's 5 minutes: the background sync leaves a booking claimed in the last 30 minutes alone whether the claim's read is still running, failed or never ended, so a read that failed (a rate limit, a timeout, an order not found) is not repeated at every scan; a refresh is held back by the same column only for its own throttle (60 s for the customer's, 10 s for the console's). The other claims that write a timed in-progress marker count as abandoned after 5 minutes, as written above: the ticketing job's (`TICKETING_CLAIM_TTL_MS`), a refund submission's (`REFUND_CLAIM_LEASE_MS`) and the notification outbox's (`CLAIM_LEASE_MS`). Accepted as built.

   **2026-10-26 (lead decision (1), FIX-W30-OTA): the customer's cancellation and the ticketing claim.** bookings.cancel refuses a booking with a fresh ticketing claim (the ticketing job's, the reconciliation's inline issuance or a staff issuance, `ticketing_status = 'in_progress'` within the 5 minutes) as console.bookings.cancel does, with the same check and answer: `409 BOOKING_NOT_MODIFIABLE`, `details.reason = ticketing_in_progress`, and nothing is sent to Amadeus or Stripe ([change-and-cancel.md](../flows/change-and-cancel.md) §3 step 4 and §4). A claim older than 5 minutes counts as abandoned and no longer holds the cancellation back.

   **2026-10-27 (lead decision (1), FIX-W31-OTA): the ticketing claims and an undecided cancellation.** The opposite race of the note above: a cancellation's claim (T1 of bookings.cancel or console.bookings.cancel) only bumped `version`, so once it had committed and its Flight Order Management DELETE was in flight, a ticketing retry that fell due (the job's `claimSkip`, which the reconciliation's inline issuance goes through too) or a staff issuance (console.bookings.issue) could still claim the booking and send Flight Order Issue to an order being deleted. T1 now also writes `bookings.cancellation_claimed_at` (tenant migration `0005`), and every outcome step clears it: the record of the cancellation, the record of a request for the staff, the flag of a DELETE without an answer, and the release of a DELETE that applied nothing (Amadeus's 2552, a request that never reached Amadeus, the airline's refusal of a staff cancellation). From T1's commit until that outcome, and while the cancellation's state is `undecided` (a DELETE without an answer until the worker's check decides it, cancelled or not applied; a flag the worker hands to the staff's review stays `undecided`, as console.bookings.issue already treated it), the ticketing job skips the booking (skip reason `cancellation_pending`: it stays due, and a later scan claims it once nothing holds it) and console.bookings.issue answers `409 BOOKING_NOT_MODIFIABLE` with `details.reason = cancellation_pending`, its refusal of an undecided cancellation (`cancellationHoldOf` in [`cancellation-state.ts`](../../backend/src/bookings/cancellation-state.ts)). Both read the column with the row their `FOR NO KEY UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` returns (T1 and every outcome step update the booking row) and the cancellation's events in a statement after that lock (the flag is a booking event, not a column of the row). A request that stopped while its DELETE was in flight leaves the column behind: a claim older than 5 minutes (`CANCELLATION_CLAIM_TTL_MS`, as long as the ticketing claim holds a cancellation back) holds nothing back, and the next ticketing attempt starts with its resync GET, which finds an order the DELETE removed (a failed attempt, never an issuance). A cancellation that ends as a request for the staff or as not applied leaves the order live, and ticketing goes on as before ([ticketing-and-documents.md](../flows/ticketing-and-documents.md) §3 step 3, [change-and-cancel.md](../flows/change-and-cancel.md) §8).

   **2026-10-28 (lead decision (1), FIX-W32-OTA): the third claim, a second cancellation, and each claim's own marker.**
   - (a) checkout.complete's own issuance (`issue_after_payment` after its capture: booking-orchestrator.service.ts `issue`) did not read the hold, so a customer's or a staff cancellation whose T1 committed between the booking's confirmation and that issuance could race it. It now reads the hold under its own row lock (`FOR UPDATE`, the column and the cancellation's events in statements after the lock: `cancellationHoldFactsOf` in [`cancellation-state.ts`](../../backend/src/bookings/cancellation-state.ts)) and, held, sends no Flight Order Issue: it makes the issuance due now (`PaymentsRepository.requestIssuanceAfterPayment`, as the payments queue's safety net makes it) and answers the `201` of the confirmed booking; the ticketing job skips the booking while the hold lasts (`cancellation_pending`) and issues it once nothing holds it. A T1 that comes after the issuance's claim skips the booking that claim holds, and then meets its fresh ticketing claim (`ticketing_in_progress`).
   - (b) bookings.cancel and console.bookings.cancel refuse while another cancellation's claim is fresh (`cancellation_claimed_at` younger than `CANCELLATION_CLAIM_TTL_MS`): `409 BOOKING_NOT_MODIFIABLE` with `details.reason = pending`, the answer both give an undecided cancellation, in T0 and again in T1 under the claim's lock. T1 bumps `version`, so a second request sent with the new ETag (another tab that reloaded the booking) passed the version check and would send a second DELETE. Every outcome step now clears only its own claim's marker, compared with the value its T1 wrote, as `releaseClaim` did already: the record of the cancellation (`markCancelled`), of a request (`bumpVersion`), the flag of a DELETE without an answer and the release of one that applied nothing; a `request_only` request, which writes no marker, and the worker's check, whose flag ended its claim, clear none. So an outcome never ends the hold of another claim.
   - (c) Confirmed as built: a DELETE without an answer that the worker hands to the staff's review at its deadline (`booking.cancellation_manual_review`) stays `undecided`, and keeps the ticketing claims (the job, console.bookings.issue, and now the completion's issuance) held until the staff settle it.
7. **Failures**: default `attempts: 5`, exponential backoff from 30 s; failed jobs kept 30 days; the platform admin's failed-job view and the on-VM check read their counts ([ADR-0016](../../../docs/mvp/adr/0016-observability.md)).
8. **Health**: `/health/ready` on port 4001 reports PostgreSQL, Redis and the age of the oldest waiting job per queue; the on-VM check alerts above 10 minutes.

## Consequences

### Positive

- One busy OTA cannot hold a queue for long; work is spread in small jobs.
- Overlapping scheduler runs and restarts are harmless.
- Suspension does not hurt travellers who already paid.
- Since 2026-10-22 a claim is never put off by a document being written or an event being inserted for its booking: only another claim or a writer of the booking makes it skip.
- Since 2026-10-27 no Flight Order Issue goes to an order whose cancellation's DELETE is in flight or got no answer: the cancellation and the ticketing claims now hold each other back both ways.
- Since 2026-10-28 that holds for checkout.complete's own issuance too, no second DELETE is sent while a cancellation's DELETE is in flight, and no outcome ends another claim's hold.

### Negative

- Fan-out jobs read the tenant list every minute; with hundreds of tenants this is many small jobs and Redis writes.
- Bounded batches mean a backlog drains over several runs after an outage.
- A completion whose issuance a cancellation held answers a confirmed booking without tickets; the tickets come with the ticketing job's next scan once the hold ends (up to 5 minutes), or never when the cancellation goes through (2026-10-28).

### Follow-ups

- Measure queue latency in the M6 performance sanity check; split the worker into two processes (booking-critical and housekeeping) if one blocks the other.

## Alternatives considered

- **One job per scheduler run for all tenants.** Simpler, but one large tenant delays all others and a crash restarts everything.
- **Cron scripts outside the worker.** No retries, no visibility ([ADR-0009](../../../docs/mvp/adr/0009-background-jobs-bullmq.md)).
- **PostgreSQL `LISTEN/NOTIFY` triggers.** Ties work to database connections and does not survive restarts.
- **Claims with `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`** (the rule before 2026-10-22). It also skips a booking whose `FOR KEY SHARE` another transaction holds, the foreign-key lock of any insert of a child row, so a booking could wait a whole scan interval for a reason that does not concern the claim.
- **Keep `FOR UPDATE` in the documents' write and retry the skipped claims sooner.** The race stays, only shorter; a lock the documents do not need for their own rows would still hold up the booking's claims.
