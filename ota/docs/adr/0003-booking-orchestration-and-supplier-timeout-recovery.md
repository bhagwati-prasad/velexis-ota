# OADR-0003: Booking orchestration and SUPPLIER_TIMEOUT recovery

**Status:** Accepted
**Date:** 2026-10-12
**Deciders:** OTA backend tech lead. Decided during planning; revisit when Amadeus offers an idempotency key or a search by remark for Flight Create Orders, or when the booking queue convention is confirmed or changed with Amadeus during M2.

## Context

`checkout.complete` turns a checkout into an Amadeus order. The contract fixes the outward behaviour: Flight Create Orders at most once per checkout, a `pending` booking and `504 SUPPLIER_TIMEOUT` when Amadeus does not answer, a reconciliation "in the queuing office (Queue List / Flight Order Management) for up to 15 minutes", then confirm or void and fail, and same-key replays of the `504` until the decision. [`conventions.md` §2](../../../docs/mvp/conventions.md#rules) allows the order-creation step to keep a transaction open across the call. Amadeus has no idempotency key on order creation and no search by remark; Queue List returns PNRs of a queue with their creation time and traveller names. The airline-card method adds a twist: the card may have been charged when the answer is lost ([ADR-0011](../../../docs/mvp/adr/0011-payments-stripe-and-airline-card.md)).

## Decision

1. **Five steps, no transaction across a call:** claim (T1), call, record (T2), money, issue ([`../architecture-booking.md`](../architecture-booking.md) §1). We do **not** use the conventions' exception: holding a row lock and a pooled connection for up to 60 s risks pool exhaustion on one VM, and a crash during the call would roll back the only trace of the attempt.
2. **The claim is a `pending` booking row** committed before the call, with `create_order_started_at`. `bookings.checkout_id` is unique, so a concurrent or repeated completion of the same checkout cannot reach Amadeus a second time; it finds the pending booking and answers like the contract says (`504` with the same details, or `409 CHECKOUT_ALREADY_COMPLETED`).

   **What is sent is recorded first** (2026-10-21, third batch). Just before Flight Create Orders, under the booking's lock, the booking must still be `pending` and undecided (a reconciliation that adopted a claim whose request took longer than its 2 minutes may have decided it: then nothing is sent), and it records what is about to be sent: `issue_requested` (the flag sent) and, after an accepted re-price (a lower or equal total), the priced offer sent with its amounts (`priced_offer_id`, the net amounts, markup, fees, extras and `total_amount`). A process that dies after the send therefore leaves a booking that holds the order's amounts, and the reconciliation of the adopted claim captures that total, never the first priced offer's, and never more than the authorisation. The checkout itself moves to the re-priced offer only once the order may exist (the record step, or an unknown outcome): a request that is not sent leaves it on the total its authorisation holds, so a retry can use that authorisation.
3. **Every order is findable.** Each Flight Create Orders request carries:
   - the general remark `VELEXIS <booking reference>` (the booking reference is generated at the claim);
   - an `automatedProcess` entry that places the PNR immediately on the tenant's booking queue (`storefront_settings.booking_queue_number` and category, seeded to queue 50 / category 0 on Velexis shared offices) in the queuing office.
4. **Reconciliation** after an unknown outcome (point 9: no answer, or an answer that does not prove a refusal, once the request may have reached Amadeus): set `reconcile_until = create_order_started_at + 15 min`, answer `504`, enqueue `booking.reconcile` (first run after 30 s, then the 1-minute scan). Each run lists the booking queue, keeps PNRs created between `create_order_started_at − 1 min` and `+ 5 min` whose names include the lead traveller's last name, reads every one of these candidates by reference, the closest in time first, and accepts only one carrying the `VELEXIS <reference>` remark.

   **Complete searches** (2026-10-21, third batch). Queue List gives creation times to the minute, so several PNRs of one lead last name can share the order's minute; reading only three of them (the rule before FIX-W25-BOOKINGS) could leave the order unread and void it as not found. Every candidate of the window is read. A safety cap of 50 reads per attempt stays; an attempt with more candidates reads 50 and the next attempt reads the next ones (in turns over the ordered list). A search that could not read every candidate is **incomplete**: the cap was reached, Queue List failed, answered something else than a list of PNRs or may have left PNRs out (it holds the `max` the reconciliation asks for, 500, or its `meta.count` says the queue holds more), an entry that may be the order has no record locator, or no traveller names that can be read (it is not read: without the lead's name it may be any other booking's PNR, possibly another OTA's), a read failed or timed out (other than a `404`), or the order with the remark cannot be read. An incomplete search never proves the order absent and never voids: the booking stays `pending` and is searched again until `reconcile_until`; only a complete search that finds no matching order proves absence. A search still incomplete at `reconcile_until` takes point 5's undecided path (after up to 3 more attempts when only transient failures left it incomplete, 2026-10-22).

   **Stale claims** (2026-10-21, second batch). A booking can stay `pending` without `reconcile_until` when the process died between the claim and Flight Create Orders, or when the unknown-outcome write itself failed (`bookings.reconcile_not_scheduled`); its checkout would stay locked (point 10) and its authorisation held until it lapses. The 1-minute scan therefore also **adopts** every `pending` booking without `reconcile_until` or `reconcile_outcome` whose create-order attempt (`create_order_started_at`, written by the claim) started **more than 2 minutes** ago: it sets `reconcile_until = now + 15 min` (the normal window, from the adoption) and the staff event `booking.reconcile_adopted`, in the scan's transaction (`FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`, `FOR NO KEY UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` since 2026-10-22, OADR-0014 point 6: so two scans adopt a claim once, and the foreign-key lock of a row being inserted for the booking never makes a scan skip it), and the booking is then reconciled like any unknown outcome. The order may exist (the request may have been sent), so it is searched for in the queuing office first, and the authorisation is voided and the checkout reopened only once it is proven absent (point 5). A claim younger than 2 minutes is left alone: its request may still be in flight (a re-price waits up to 20 s per Flight Offers Price call, Flight Create Orders up to 60 s). Should a request still be running when its claim is adopted, the reconciliation and the request both decide only under the booking's lock and only while it is `pending`, so the first decision stands and Flight Create Orders is still sent at most once.
5. **Decisions.**

   | Outcome | Stripe | Airline card |
   |---|---|---|
   | Found | Record the order (step 3), capture the booking's total (point 2: the priced offer sent; never more than the authorisation), issue per policy, confirmation e-mail | Record the order and tickets, confirmation e-mail |
   | Not found by `reconcile_until` (a complete search, point 4) | Void the authorisation, booking `failed` (`failure_code = SUPPLIER_TIMEOUT`), checkout reopened if not expired, e-mail `booking.not_confirmed`, staff alert | **No automatic failure**: `ticketing_status = manual_review`, `reconcile_outcome = manual_review`, staff alert, traveller told the booking is being confirmed |
   | Search still incomplete at `reconcile_until` (2026-10-21, third batch) | **No void**: the booking stays `pending` with its checkout (still locked) and its authorisation, `ticketing_status = manual_review`, `reconcile_outcome = manual_review` (the scan stops), staff event `booking.reconcile_manual_review`, staff alert; the staff decide | Manual review |
   | No booking queue configured | Same as "not found" after 15 minutes, with the alert saying the office must be checked by hand | Manual review |

   **Grace after the deadline** (2026-10-22, lead decision (3), FIX-W26-BOOKINGS). A search at or after `reconcile_until` that is incomplete **only because of transient failures** (a rate limit, a timeout, a `5xx` or a network error of Queue List or of a by-reference read: the API codes `RATE_LIMITED`, `SUPPLIER_TIMEOUT`, `SUPPLIER_UNAVAILABLE`, `SERVICE_UNAVAILABLE`, or an Amadeus `5xx`, `429`, timeout or network error behind another code) gets **up to 3 more attempts**, one per reconcile run (`RECONCILE_DEADLINE_RETRIES`), before it takes the "search still incomplete" row above: each such attempt writes the staff event `booking.reconcile_deadline_retry` (their number is the grace used; no column is needed) and leaves the booking `pending` without an outcome, and the next attempt decides like any attempt past the deadline (the order found is recorded, a complete search that finds nothing proves it absent, a fourth transient failure goes to the staff's review). A search incomplete for any other reason (the read cap still reached after the rotation, a Queue List that may be truncated or cannot be read, entries or an order that cannot be read, a read Amadeus refuses) goes to the staff's review at the deadline as before, and a complete search decides as before. A rate limit or a timeout of the minute of the deadline therefore no longer sends a booking to the staff that the next minute would have decided.

6. **Replays.** The idempotency record of a timed-out completion points at the booking (`resource_id`); replays read the booking state and return the same `504` until `reconcile_outcome` is set, then the final result ([`../architecture-booking.md`](../architecture-booking.md) §5).
7. **Same pattern for other writes.** Issue: on timeout no retry in the request; the ticketing job resyncs before any retry. Cancel (DELETE): booking flagged, GET within 15 minutes, then the money step once. Patch: resync, then the caller retries with `If-Match`.
8. **Late discoveries.** If staff later find an order that reconciliation missed, they import it from the console (`console.bookings.import`) and settle payment with the traveller; the failed booking stays for audit.
9. **What an answer of Flight Create Orders proves.** Only an answer that proves the order was not created may fail the booking. A failed call is one of three kinds (`createFailureKind` in [`booking-orchestrator.service.ts`](../../backend/src/bookings/booking-orchestrator.service.ts)):

   | Kind | Answers | Booking | Authorisation | Checkout | API answer |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | **Refused**: Amadeus processed the request and created nothing | An Amadeus `4xx` whose mapped code is a business refusal: `SOLD_OUT` (34651), `PRICE_CHANGED` (37200), `TRAVELER_DATA_INVALID` or `SUPPLIER_REJECTED` (4926, 32171, and any other `4xx` but `401`, `403` and `429`), `TICKETING_FAILED` (9112), `PAYMENT_DECLINED` (1304), `VERSION_CONFLICT` (2552) | `failed`, `checkout_id` cleared | Voided (`pi:cancel:<pay_id>`; a failed void queues `payment.void`) | `open` again | `409 SOLD_OUT`, `409 PRICE_CHANGED`, `422 TRAVELER_DATA_INVALID`, `502 TICKETING_FAILED`, else `502 SUPPLIER_ERROR` |
   | **Not sent**: the request never reached the order | An error raised before the request left the backend (the OAuth token request, the token buckets, Redis, the connection and office checks, an unexpected error); a connection that was never made (`ECONNREFUSED`, `ENOTFOUND`, `EAI_AGAIN`, `EHOSTUNREACH`, `ENETUNREACH`, `UND_ERR_CONNECT_TIMEOUT` and the TLS certificate errors); an Amadeus `401` (after the client's one retry with a new token), `403` or `429` without a business code, which refuse the request before processing it | `failed`, `checkout_id` cleared | Kept | Still `payment_pending`; a new completion may send the order | The error in the operation's codes, such as `503 SUPPLIER_UNAVAILABLE`, `503 SERVICE_UNAVAILABLE`, `424 SUPPLIER_NOT_CONFIGURED`, `429 RATE_LIMITED` or `502 SUPPLIER_ERROR` |
   | **Unknown**: the order may exist | Every other answer once the request may have reached Amadeus: no answer in time, a reset or otherwise cut connection, an Amadeus `5xx` (`500` with 141 SYSTEM ERROR, `502`, `503`, `504` and any other), an answer that cannot be read (a `2xx` that is not JSON, any other status, a created order whose answer cannot be parsed), and a created order whose recording (T2) fails | Stays `pending` with its `checkout_id`; `reconcile_until = create_order_started_at + 15 min`; event `booking.supplier_timeout` | Kept: never voided by the request | `payment_pending`, locked (point 10) | `504 SUPPLIER_TIMEOUT` (`details.bookingId`) |

   Once a request of the checkout may have reached Amadeus, Flight Create Orders is never sent again for that checkout: a refused booking is `failed`, and an unknown one stays `pending` until the reconciliation (points 4 and 5) decides it. Before FIX-W22 an Amadeus `500` counted as a refusal and a `502` or `503` as not sent, so the authorisation of an order that existed could be voided, or the order sent again. The airline-card completion (OBE-051) classifies its answers the same way, with the money of point 5 ([`../flows/checkout-airline-card.md`](../flows/checkout-airline-card.md) §4).
10. **The checkout lock.** While a checkout has a `pending` booking (checkout.complete claimed it: the order is being created, or is looked for after a `504`), nothing of the checkout changes. Its mutating steps, `checkout.setTravelers`, `checkout.setContacts`, `checkout.setRemarks`, `checkout.setAncillaries`, `checkout.createPayment` and `checkout.updatePayment`, answer `409 CHECKOUT_BEING_COMPLETED` (`details.bookingId`). They check it under the checkout's row lock, which the claim takes too, so a change and a claim never overlap; the steps that call Amadeus or Stripe first also check before that call. `checkout.complete` and `checkout.completeWithAirlineCard` answer the booking's `504 SUPPLIER_TIMEOUT` (point 2). So no new payment replaces, and voids, the authorisation the booking is made with, and the travellers, extras and amounts stay those of the order that may exist. A job that expires checkouts (`checkouts.expire`, and the suspension's expiry of [OADR-0007](0007-tenant-migration-runner-and-provisioning-worker.md) point 8) skips a checkout whose booking is `pending`: its authorisation stays for the reconciliation, the only one that voids or captures it. The lock ends when the reconciliation decides (point 5); a booking whose search was still incomplete at `reconcile_until` keeps it until the staff settle the booking (2026-10-21, third batch). With a Stripe payment: found, the booking is recorded and the checkout `completed`; not found, the authorisation is voided through `ota-payments`, the booking is `failed` with its `checkout_id` cleared, and the checkout is reopened if it has not expired, else `checkouts.expire` expires it on its next run.
11. **Lock order** (2026-10-21, second batch; extended by the third batch below). Every transaction that locks both a booking and its payment locks the **booking first, then the payment**, so two such transactions never wait for each other in a circle (a deadlock that PostgreSQL would break by aborting one). This holds for the steps of point 1 (the claim, the failure, the capture and its failure), the reconciliation's decisions, and the payment jobs of `ota-payments` (the capture retry shows its capture on the booking). A transaction that holds a payment's lock and only writes a row that references the booking (an outbox alert, a booking event, a refund) takes the booking's `FOR KEY SHARE` first too, because that row's foreign key takes it at the end of the insert anyway: the authorisation watch, a dispute's alert, a failed refund's alert and a refund's request (`RefundsRepository.create`).

    **2026-10-21, third batch (lead decision, FIX-W25-BOOKINGS).** The full order is the **checkout, then the booking, then the payment, then the refund**, and a foreign-key lock counts as a lock of the parent: the `FOR KEY SHARE` that an insert (or a change of the foreign-key column) takes on the row it references comes in the order where that parent comes. So:
    - the transactions after Flight Create Orders (the record step, the failure that reopens the checkout, the unknown outcome that moves it to a re-priced offer) and the reconciliation's decisions (found, not found) lock the checkout first, as the claim does; before FIX-W25-BOOKINGS the not-found decision locked the booking, then the payments, then the checkout, and the record step and the unknown outcome the booking before the checkout;
    - `refund.submit`'s transactions that write a payment event or an alert (the claim, the record of the Stripe refund, a refusal) take the payment's booking and the payment `FOR KEY SHARE` before the refund (`RefundsRepository.lockParents`), as the webhook's refund transaction locks the payment, then its refunds; before, the job locked the refund and its payment event's insert key-shared the payment after it, the reverse of the webhook's order;
    - a row the transaction inserted itself counts as locked from its insert, since no other transaction can see or lock it before the commit: the claim locks the checkout, an existing booking, then the payment, and the payment's `booking_id` then references the booking the claim inserted.

    Each transaction's locks, in order, are listed in [`../architecture-booking.md`](../architecture-booking.md) §1.1; the tests hold the earlier row in a second transaction and check that the later one is free while the code waits ([`lock-order.ts`](../../backend/test/helpers/lock-order.ts)).

    **2026-10-22 (lead decision (1), FIX-W26-BOOKINGS).** A claim of a booking (the ticketing job's and the reconciliation's inline issuance, the reconciliation's claim, the scan's adoption, the sync) locks it `FOR NO KEY UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` (OADR-0014 point 6), not `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`: it is the booking's lock in the order above, and the `FOR KEY SHARE` that a foreign key of another transaction's insert takes on the booking no longer makes it skip. The booking documents' write (and their failed row) take a transaction-level advisory lock keyed on the booking id first, then the booking's `FOR KEY SHARE` (the version they check), then their own `booking_documents` rows; they lock no checkout, payment or refund, and the advisory lock is taken by no other transaction, so it adds no circle to the order.

## Consequences

### Positive

- A crash at any point leaves a durable trace (the pending booking) that the reconciler or staff can act on.
- At most one order per checkout, proven by a database constraint instead of in-memory locks.
- The airline-card path never fails a booking whose card may have been charged.
- No authorisation is voided and no order is sent twice while the order may exist: an order Amadeus created behind a `5xx` or a gateway's `502`/`503` is found and confirmed by the reconciliation instead of being left unpaid.
- While the outcome is unknown the order, the booking and the checkout cannot drift apart: the checkout lock keeps its travellers, extras, amounts and authorisation as they were sent.
- A claim that nobody decides (a process that died before Flight Create Orders, an unknown-outcome write that failed) no longer locks its checkout and holds its authorisation until Stripe releases it: the first scan after its 2 minutes adopts it, and the reconciliation decides it 15 minutes after the adoption (plus one run), within about 19 minutes of the claim.
- Two transactions that lock the same booking and payment wait for each other in one order only, so none is aborted as a deadlock; since the third batch the same holds for a checkout and its booking, and for a payment and its refunds.
- An order is never voided because the search could not read it: every PNR of the window with the lead's name is read, however many share the order's minute, and a search that could not read them all never proves the order absent.
- A claim adopted after a re-price is captured for the total of the order that was sent, as a booking decided by the request is.
- A rate limit or a timeout in the minute of the deadline no longer sends a booking to the staff that the next minute would have decided (2026-10-22, point 5's grace).
- A reconciliation or ticketing claim is never put off by a document or an event being written for its booking (2026-10-22, point 11).

### Negative

- Every order goes to a queue, so the booking queue fills; staff or a later job must clean it (Queue List has no removal in the 12 operations). The queue is dedicated to Velexis bookings so it does not mix with other work.
- The queue convention (queue 50) is an assumption to confirm with Amadeus for the shared offices; OTAs on their own credentials must configure a queue or accept manual handling after timeouts.
- A booking can be `failed` while a PNR exists (if reconciliation misses it); the PNR's own time limit cancels it because it is unticketed and unpaid, and staff are alerted.
- An Amadeus `5xx` that created nothing still holds the traveller's authorisation and locks the checkout until the reconciliation decides (15 minutes after the request, plus one run); the storefront shows its "we are confirming your booking" page meanwhile.
- An adopted claim is decided 15 minutes after its adoption (plus one run), so up to about 19 minutes after the claim (the adoption comes with the first scan after its 2 minutes); a claim whose request never reached Amadeus costs the same wait as an unknown outcome, because the backend cannot tell the two apart.
- A booking whose process died after an accepted re-price (point 2's lower price) held the first priced offer's amounts until the third batch, so the order found was captured for the total of the authorisation, not the lower total of the re-price. Since 2026-10-21 (FIX-W25-BOOKINGS) the booking records the priced offer sent and its amounts before Flight Create Orders (point 2), and the reconciliation captures the re-priced total. What remains: the checkout of such a claim keeps the first priced offer's totals (it moves only once the order may exist, and the process died first), so a found order completes a checkout whose totals differ from the booking's; the booking and its payment hold the amounts that count.
- A booking queue that holds more PNRs than one Queue List answer (500) makes every search incomplete: timed-out bookings then go to the staff's review instead of being voided, until the queue is cleaned (follow-ups).

  **2026-10-23 (lead decision (5), FIX-W27-SYNC).** Cleaning the booking queue stays manual: Velexis has no removal among the 12 Amadeus operations, so staff remove the PNRs of finished bookings by hand in Amadeus ([runbook](../../backend/README.md#runbook-cleaning-the-booking-queue)). To warn them before a queue outgrows one answer, the reconciliation raises a staff alert (`staff.manual_review`, kind `booking_queue_nearly_full`, with the count and the 500 limit) when a Queue List answer of the booking queue holds 400 or more entries, 80 % of the 500, or its `meta.count` says the queue does, at most once a day per OTA (one dedupe key per UTC day); the search goes on as without it. The cost is a manual routine for each OTA's staff (or Velexis ops on the shared offices), and an alert that comes only with a reconciliation, so a queue that fills while no answer is lost is noticed at the next timeout.
- A search that keeps failing transiently at the deadline is decided up to 3 minutes later (the grace of point 5, 2026-10-22), and goes to the staff's review only after the fourth transient failure; its checkout stays locked and its authorisation held for those minutes.
- More PNRs of one name in the window cost more by-reference reads (up to 50 per attempt) against the Amadeus quota.

- The console's booking actions (OBE-062 and later) settle a booking in `reconcile_outcome = manual_review`: import or record its order and capture, or cancel it and release the authorisation.
- M4: a console "booking queue" view to spot orphan PNRs.
- **2026-10-23 (lead decision (5), FIX-W27-SYNC): the booking queue is cleaned by hand.** No job removes PNRs from the booking queue (Queue List cannot); staff follow the [runbook](../../backend/README.md#runbook-cleaning-the-booking-queue), alerted once a day per OTA while a reconciliation's Queue List answer holds 400 entries or more (`BOOKING_QUEUE_ALERT_ENTRIES`, `bookingQueueAlertKey` in [`reconcile.service.ts`](../../backend/src/bookings/reconcile.service.ts)). Revisit when Amadeus offers a removal, or when the M4 queue view exists.
- **2026-10-23 (lead decision (3), FIX-W27-SYNC): the booking sync and the refreshes.** The background sync (OBE-045) and the refreshes (bookings.get, getTicketingStatus and getFareRules with a refresh, console.bookings.get with `refresh=true`, console.bookings.resync) claim a booking by writing `bookings.sync_claimed_at` (tenant migration `0003`), no longer the staff-only event `booking.sync_requested`, about four a day per upcoming booking and one an hour near the deadline, which filled the console's timeline; the same 30-minute retry and the same coordination hold (a refresh within its throttle of a sync's claim stands back, a refresh's claim keeps the sync away for 30 minutes), and the events written before stay. Every path that stores a fetched order goes through the sync's diff (`order-diff.ts`, `BookingSyncService`) and writes the same events: `booking.schedule_changed` asks for a new itinerary ([OADR-0013](0013-notifications-and-booking-documents.md) point 6), and a cancellation by the airline of a booking with an authorised or captured payment raises a staff alert (the reconciler's alert writer, once per booking) besides `booking.cancelled`.
- OBE-043: the reconciler decides every `pending` booking with `reconcile_until` and so releases the checkout lock of point 10; until it is merged, a pending booking keeps its checkout locked and its authorisation held. FIX-W24-BOOKINGS adds the adoption of stale claims (point 4), so a `pending` booking without `reconcile_until` is decided too.
- Every new transaction that locks a booking and its payment (the console's capture and issue, cancellations and refunds: OBE-049, OBE-068) follows point 11; `PaymentsRepository.lockBookings` takes the booking's lock for payment code, and `RefundsRepository.create` takes it itself.
- OBE-056: `checkouts.expire` skips checkouts whose booking is `pending` (point 10). The suspension's expiry (OBE-074) must skip them too.

## Alternatives considered

- **Hold the transaction and row lock during the call** (the conventions' exception). Simpler code, but loses the trace on crash and ties up pool connections.
- **Retry Flight Create Orders after a timeout.** Can create duplicate PNRs and, with the airline card, charge the card twice.
- **Poll Flight Order Management only.** Needs the order id, which a lost answer never delivered.
- **Store the card to retry later.** Rejected by ADR-0011 (PCI requirement 3).
- **Count an Amadeus `5xx` as a refusal, or a `502` or `503` as not sent** (the behaviour before FIX-W22). A system or gateway error does not say whether the order was created behind it: voiding then leaves an order without money, and a retry may create a second one.
- **Let the checkout change while its booking is pending.** A new payment would cancel the authorisation the booking is made with, and travellers or extras changed after the request would no longer match the order the reconciliation finds.
- **Expire the checkout and void its authorisation on schedule.** The order may exist: voiding before the reconciliation decides is the same risk as voiding on a `5xx`.
- **Fail and void a stale claim at once** (instead of adopting it). The process may have died after Flight Create Orders was sent, so the order may exist: the same risk as voiding on a `5xx`.
- **Adopt a claim at once, or after 30 seconds.** Its request may still be waiting for Amadeus (up to 60 s for Flight Create Orders, after a re-price whose Flight Offers Price times out after 20 s and is sent at most twice); 2 minutes is longer than these calls take together, and a request still running when its claim is adopted is safe (point 4).
- **Lock the payment first.** Either order prevents deadlocks if every transaction follows it; the booking first matches the orchestration, whose claim and decisions start from the booking, and the reconciliation, which finds the payments through it.
- **Read at most three candidates** (the rule before the third batch). Cheap, but Queue List's minute makes PNRs of one name in the order's minute tie, and the order could stay unread and be voided as not found.
- **Keep searching an incomplete search past `reconcile_until`** until one completes (the behaviour before FIX-W25-BOOKINGS). No staff decision is asked for while the booking and its authorisation stay undecided, and a complete search hours later could still void an authorisation the contract promised to settle within 15 minutes.
- **Remember the candidates already read between attempts**, so a search can complete over several attempts. It needs a store of record locators of other bookings' (possibly other OTAs') PNRs; the cap is far above a booking queue's candidates, so the attempts read them in turns and only an attempt that read them all proves absence.
- **Move the checkout to the re-priced offer before the send.** A request that is then not sent would leave the checkout on a lower total than its authorisation, and the next completion would refuse that authorisation (`409 PAYMENT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`).
